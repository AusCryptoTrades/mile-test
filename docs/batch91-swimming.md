## Scientific basis

| Decision | Evidence and application |
| --- | --- |
| CSS calculation | The two-distance estimate fits the slope of distance against time. CSS in m/s is 200 ÷ (400 m trial seconds − 200 m trial seconds). Pace per 100 m is half the time difference. For 420 s and 200 s, CSS is 110 s/100 m or 1:50/100 m. Two-distance swimming tests have shown useful repeatability in trained swimmers. A CSS estimate can differ from maximal lactate steady state. Pool length, turns, stroke and trial pacing affect comparability. [Dekerle et al. validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11842355/) · [Stroke-specific reliability study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38380294/) · [CSS and lactate steady state](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16195984/) |
| Trial eligibility | Use completed freestyle trials over 400 m and 200 m on separate well-recovered days, in the same supervised 20, 25 or 50 m pool, from a push. Both distances must finish at the wall. The app requires normal recovery, at least 800 m continuous ability, experience beyond beginner and explicit confirmation of comparable recent trials. These eligibility and plausibility ranges are conservative product heuristics. Build never schedules maximal trials. |
| Progression and taper | Use confirmed weekly metres, time caps, longest recent swim and continuous ability where supplied. Recovery and taper use reduced planned volume. Established swimmers can receive a hard pool session after base training when the rest of their week allows it. Phase caps, five-percent/300 m progression limits and the taper's omission of hard work are coaching defaults for review, not inferred responses or universal prescriptions. [Elite swimmer systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33952709/) · [Swimmer overload and taper study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24421726/) · [Taper meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/) |
| Technique and fallback | Separate technique, easy swim, race skills and interval objectives. Use short repeats, regular breathing, relaxed stroke cues and enough wall rest. Effort bands on a 1–10 scale remain usable without CSS. The pace bands and timing fallbacks are planning estimates, not measured physiology. [Foster et al. on subjective intensity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35507232/) |
| Concurrent training | Use scheduled minutes and known hard work as scheduling signals. Limit swimming near hard or upper-body training, and suppress dedicated hard swim sessions when another sport is primary. Metres, cycling TSS and running kilometres remain separate. [Concurrent training review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/) |

The 52 pool recipes vary effort, repetition distance, rest, technique cue and equipment requirements. Canonical distance totals exclude rests. Estimated elapsed time includes timed wall rests and planned swimming at the slow end of the target or a conservative planning value. Estimated time is not guaranteed. Pool lengths are 20, 25, 33 and 50 whole metres. An open-water session uses time and effort with no distance estimate. It substitutes for a pool slot and requires established ability and organiser/buddy confirmation. Suitable conditions and in-person supervision still determine whether the session goes ahead. Pool skills substitute for missing supervision. Use regular breathing throughout. [Royal Life Saving Australia](https://www.royallifesaving.com.au/)
