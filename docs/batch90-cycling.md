# Batch 90 cycling review

Cycling uses the five-stage Build flow, the authenticated training document, the shared structured workout validator and Calendar. Running retains its existing coaching and prescription rules. Cycling workouts are timed, with 52 purpose-led prescriptions covering easy riding, pacing, cadence, endurance, threshold, repeatable high efforts and event rehearsal. Sets vary in effort, recovery, sequencing and terrain or position focus. Calendar expands the same prescription shown in Build. Editing the instructions invalidates structured presentation and requires fresh confirmation before an edited workout can be compared.

## Coaching evidence and implementation

| Topic | Implementation and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Progression and endurance | Begin with confirmed sustainable time, available days and the longest recent ride. Riders with unconfirmed capacity get short conversational rides and no automatic progression. Interruption or reported poor recovery favours a lighter block. Feasible build weeks can add up to 12 minutes, with further percentage and achieved-plan caps. The increments and eligibility checks are conservative coaching heuristics based on planned workload. [ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/) |
| Beginner intensity | Normal easy aerobic work uses RPE 2–4/10 and comfortable speech when no appropriate sensor target is available. RPE 1–2 is for warm-up, cool-down, recovery or a deliberately light ride. Neither number is a personalised physiological threshold. A recreational rider with established time and normal recovery can get introductory tempo, subject to scheduling and event gates. [Foster et al. talk-test and RPE review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35507232/) |
| Specificity and intervals | Goal and phase constrain the available recipes. Criterium work can include surges and settling. Time-trial work stresses sustainable output and position. Long-event rehearsals emphasise pacing, terrain and familiar fuelling. Different work-to-rest structures can be appropriate in trained cyclists. The recipe doses and rotation are coaching choices pending independent evaluation. [Seiler et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21812820/) · [Rønnestad et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24382021/) |
| Distribution and fatigue | Most cycling time is low intensity. The generator allows at most one demanding ride per week and protects the long ride and other hard days. Repeated self-reported fatigue can justify reviewing a single easier replacement. There is no readiness conclusion from one hard session or from a power-based stress estimate. [Stöggl and Sperlich](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24550842/) · [Limits of the Banister model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1974899/) |
| Recovery and taper | The policy caps recovery, peak and taper weeks at 72%, 85% and 58% of their planned weekly load before subtracting conflicts. Low-capacity slots and event dates can cut actual time further. Hard sets are withheld from peak and taper weeks in this conservative beta approach. Standard taper studies often retain some intensity, which remains a coaching review item. [Bosquet et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/) · [Endurance taper review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/) |
| Concurrent training | Cycling keeps sport-specific units separate. A day on or adjacent to a known hard or lower-body session has at most a 30-minute easy spin. Prefer a clear day for the long ride. Hold progression for non-cycling priorities and when known other-sport time equals or exceeds cycling load. This is scheduling guidance. Integrated triathlon, brick training and a shared physiological stress score require separate work. [Schumann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/) |
| Power profiling and FTP | Best contiguous 5 s, 1 min, 5 min and 20 min averages describe recorded efforts. Dated FTP has a source, method and confidence. The optional sustained benchmark needs established training, normal recovery, power equipment, athlete opt-in and an appropriate slot. MILE leaves FTP changes to the athlete and uses no universal 95%-of-20-min conversion. [Power-duration profiling review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34708276/) · [Borszcz et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29801189/) |

### Calculations and interval confidence

- Cycling power zone upper bounds are 55%, 75%, 90%, 105%, 120% and 150% of FTP. Integer bounds round down, and the next zone begins one watt higher. Sweet spot is an overlapping 88–94% coaching band. Short accelerations use effort and recovery cues without FTP-derived sprint wattages. Cycling HR targets require athlete-confirmed cycling ranges. Short efforts use RPE rather than targeting HR. [Coggan power levels](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/power-training-levels/)
- Resample at 1 Hz using sample-and-hold for intervals up to three seconds. Recorded zero watts count as coasting. Pause seconds are excluded. Invalid or missing values truncate rolling windows. Normalised power requires five minutes, 90% active power coverage and at least 80% of the possible 30-second windows. NP is the fourth root of the mean of the fourth powers of rolling 30-second means. IF is NP divided by dated FTP. The power-based stress estimate is moving hours multiplied by IF² × 100. The sampled active span must agree within 1% or three seconds. VI uses measured average power as the divisor. [Coggan definitions](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/normalized-power-intensity-factor-training-stress/)
- Zone totals use observed seconds. Decoupling compares watts per bpm across equal halves of a steady indoor ride of at least 30 minutes, excluding five minutes at each end. It requires complete pairs, VI no higher than 1.06, and power CV no higher than 12%. Heat, hydration or sensor issues may affect the result. Treat those quality thresholds as reviewable heuristics.
- An athlete-confirmed Calendar link captures the exact canonical step snapshot and revision. MILE IQ compares timed cues only if that snapshot still agrees and the recording's start, elapsed span and active span agree with the activity summary. Explicit pauses leave the cue clock. Dropouts remain missing on that clock. An unlabelled clock discrepancy suppresses all cue comparisons. Partial and unreached cues are marked individually and excluded from complete-cue counts. Calculations use at least 90% valid target coverage and exclude the first 60 seconds of HR cues. No step-level RPE is inferred from a whole-session note.
- Planned-clock alignment is an estimate. It does not confirm when the athlete pressed lap, shifted a cue, or followed the target. Legacy links without a prescription snapshot can compare totals only. Duplicate links and edited or non-timed prescriptions cannot receive cue scores. IQ keeps the quantitative cue summary short and places coverage-sensitive details behind Detailed Analysis.

## Approval, ownership and audit history

Cycling reuses the private proposal table, authenticated RPCs, training-record lock and consent mechanism. The server inspects the two latest saved fatigue check-ins within seven calendar dates. Both must be 7–10/10 and the newest must be recent. Older ratings cannot override a newer low or malformed rating. The current policy considers the next visible ride strictly in the future and within seven days. It requires a unique, intact, generated, uncompleted and unmatched demanding cycling workout with no Garmin publication record. Review returns an exact before and after. Approval expires after 20 minutes and rechecks evidence, date and the whole workout under the same lock. Leaving the review or choosing Keep current ride leaves the workout as scheduled.

The write records the exact approved snapshot atomically. Repeating approval returns its existing receipt. The same evidence day or workout cannot produce two applied revisions. A stale document cannot overwrite an approved prescription or revert completion. Athletes may complete, skip or add notes to an approved ride. They may hide its programme from Calendar. Approved history shows the latest ten entries to that athlete and omits private notes. Reviews do not change FTP or publish to Garmin. The approval UI is staging-gated. Automatic adaptation and Garmin republication stay disabled.

## Synthetic coaching review

The existing 34-athlete matrix covers 1,088 boundary scenarios. A separate review runs 128 complete programmes using 32 supported fixtures at 8, 12, 16 and 24 weeks, with a race after the final week where applicable. It checks exact seconds, scheduled dates, load and taper bounds, interval dose, variety, warm-up and cool-down and hard-day separation. The remaining two profiles cover invalid HR and injury inputs. These are engineering and rule checks. A qualified cycling coach still needs to review dose, novice progression and taper choices, especially for competitive, long-event and returning athletes. Hosted two-account privacy, real recordings, a physical mobile keyboard and Garmin sandbox and device checks are release gates.

| Cyclist | Example | Scheduled rides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner, no sensors | Base week, 75 min | Two aerobic rides of 23 and 22 min, plus a 30 min starting-and-shifting ride. The main segments are conversational, around RPE 2–4. |
| Recreational, HR 115–139 bpm for easy rides | Base week, 196 min | Two skills or pacing rides of 55 and 58 min, and an 83 min endurance ride with pacing checks. Sustained easy segments use the confirmed cycling HR range. |
| Competitive criterium rider, 240 W FTP | Build week 3, 291 min | 55 and 58 min of supporting work, a 58 min threshold workout and a 120 min long ride. Threshold work is 3 × 6 min at 228–245 W with 3 min recovery before the next repeat. |
| Running-primary athlete with a demanding Wednesday run | Base week, 138 min cycling | 55 and 83 min of aerobic work. The Wednesday cycling slot is displaced by the scheduled run and receives no extra volume the next day. |

**Example MILE IQ.** A synthetic trainer recording confirms 60 min against 60 min planned. Two complete 10-minute cues averaged 200 W inside a 185–215 W target. Aim for similar pacing in the next comparable set. Detailed Analysis marks the boundaries as estimated and shows the measured seconds in target. A steady one-hour recording at 200 W against a dated FTP of 250 W gives NP 200 W, IF 0.80, stress 64 and VI 1.00.
